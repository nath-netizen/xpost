# Voice — product surface

Adapted from the author's established register. Note the difference from social
posts: a product is used in a hard moment, often by someone dysregulated, sometimes
read aloud by a parent to a child. Warmth matters more; cleverness matters less.

## Who is speaking

A clinician-and-builder who is part of the neurodivergent community, not a brand
talking at it. Warm, direct, unsentimental. Speaks with people, never down to them.
Credible without sounding clinical.

## On the page

- Plainspoken. Short sentences. No throat-clearing, no preamble paragraph.
- Second person, not imperative drill-sergeant. "You can stop here" over "Complete
  all sections."
- Give permission to skip. A worksheet that demands completion is a demand, and in
  this niche that is a design failure, not a motivation problem.
- Low-demand by construction: entry points anywhere, no required order, no streaks,
  no guilt copy, no "day 1 of 30".
- Affirming without saccharine. Respect the reader's intelligence.
- Dry humor is welcome in small doses. No quirky-brand voice. Emoji 0–1, usually 0.

## Design register

- High contrast, generous whitespace, no visual noise. Sensory load is a real cost.
- Dyslexia- and low-vision-friendly type. Do not rely on color alone to carry meaning.
- Printer-honest: offer an ink-light version. A 40-page full-bleed PDF is a hostile
  product.
- Fillable PDF where it saves the buyer from printing at all.
- Legible at phone size if it could plausibly be read on a phone.

## Never

- Functioning labels, cure framing, pity, inspiration porn.
- Compliance and reward-chart mechanics.
- Medical advice, diagnosis, or treatment claims.
- Manufactured urgency, fake scarcity, or "link in bio" energy.
